Ambition and the frontier

Ambition and the frontier

Based on Felix Rieseberg, “How to be more ambitious: A tutorial for humans with models”

To discuss:

What’s the most ambitious successful use of AI you’ve seen?

This could be in your own work, or out in the world.

Rieseberg, on ambition:

  • Spend some of your time on tasks the model can’t do yet.
  • The next model probably can, and you’ll be ready.

Four ways to be more ambitious

  • Speed

    Do it much faster than you thought reasonable.

  • Altitude

    Ask for the outcome, not the task.

  • Depth

    Push until an expert could learn something from the result.

  • Range

    Bring in more fields than you’d normally pay for.

Adapted from Felix Rieseberg, “How to be more ambitious”

Speed

Speed is the bread and butter of AI right now: do what you’ve been doing, but several times faster, with fewer human bottlenecks.

We won’t belabor this.

    Depth

    Push until an expert could learn something from the result.

    Depth

    You’ve asked, “Why are loyalty members visiting less?” Which of these would a top analyst add to the request? Pick all that apply.

      Start from the raw transaction data, not a summary.

      A summary has already decided what matters.

      Test whether it’s really a seasonal dip before explaining it.

      Check the pattern before you explain it.

      Check the answer against last year’s data you haven’t seen.

      An outside judge catches what the model would miss.

      Make it sound confident.

      That changes the tone, not the thinking.

      Keep it to three bullet points.

      That’s a format choice. It doesn’t add depth.

      Find the earliest signal that a member is about to lapse.

      Looking earlier in the data is depth.

      A deeper request asks for the raw material, tests the assumption, and checks the answer against something outside the model.

      More than role-play

      Naming a role

      “You are the world’s best food scientist.”

      Sounds like a food scientist.

      Naming the methods

      “Use the methods the world’s best food scientist would use: blind taste panels, shelf-life tests, cost per cup.”

      Works like one.

      Naming a role mostly changes the tone. Naming the methods changes what the model actually does.

      Make your problem more ambitious

      Four ways to raise the ambition

      1. Speed

        Reply to every candidate within an hour, without adding recruiters.

      2. Altitude

        Make candidates feel looked after from first message to first day.

      3. Depth

        Find which messages actually change whether a candidate accepts, and spend recruiter time only there.

      4. Range

        What would a hotel concierge, a sales-development rep, and a support lead each change about how we talk to candidates?
